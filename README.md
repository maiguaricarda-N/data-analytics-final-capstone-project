# data-analytics-final-capstone-project
Project brief

Africa Is Rising: Are Its Women Rising Too?

Project idea
Many African economies have grown over the past three decades. I would like to understand whether women have shared in that growth. This project looks at what the available data can tell us about women's work, income, equality and safety across African countries, and where the data is silent. I do not assume that economic growth improves women's lives, and I will be careful not to claim more than the evidence supports.

Topics I will explore
1.	Economic growth: how income per person has changed in each African country.
2.	Women at work: how many women are in the labour force, and how this compares with men.
3.	Women and men side by side: income, schooling and health, where data exists.
4.	Equality: how gender inequality scores have changed over time.
5.	Safety: what homicide statistics show for women and for men.
6.	Who kills women: what is known about perpetrators, in the few countries that record it.
7.	Attitudes: what surveys say about how women view violence against them.
8.	Missing data: which countries report little or nothing, and what that means.
Objectives
1.	To show how income has changed across African countries since 1990.
2.	To explore women's labour-force participation and compare it with men's.
3.	To compare women's and men's income, schooling and health.
4.	To describe female homicide and what is known about perpetrators, without treating every killing as femicide.
5.	To show how reporting differs between countries, and how much we can safely conclude.
6.	To give recommendations that the evidence supports.


Plan
I will begin by collecting data from trusted international sources: the World Bank, the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime, UNDP and the International Labour Organization. I will keep the original files unchanged. I will then clean the data, keep only African countries, and combine the sources by country and year, leaving missing values as missing. Next I will study trends, compare countries and look for relationships, without treating any relationship as proof of cause. Finally, I will present the findings in an interactive dashboard, with a report explaining the method and the limits of the data.

Deliverables

The original data files, kept untouched.
Cleaned and checked datasets, with one combined table ready for analysis.
A data dictionary explaining every column and unit.
A written record of how the data was collected, cleaned and combined.
Charts and summaries showing trends and comparisons.
An interactive dashboard.
A report on the method and the limits of the data.
A final data story with recommendations.

A note on limits
Reporting differs from country to country, and under-reporting is likely, particularly for killings of women.There are limitations as not all the dataset have all the data.
